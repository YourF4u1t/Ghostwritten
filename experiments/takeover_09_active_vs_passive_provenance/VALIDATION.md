# Validation — Takeover 09

## Verdict

**PASS / B-grade corrective boundary; mechanism hypothesis rejected.** Active
provenance retrieval did not recreate the earlier self-versus-other behavioral
gate even though source interpretation, tool use, and the donor behavioral
window were all verified.

## Execution and integrity

- 100/100 episodes completed; five cells each contain n=20.
- No API errors, runner errors, partial records, retries, or exclusions.
- Active cells called provenance in 40/40 trials; passive cells avoided the tool
  in 40/40.
- Final source labels were jointly correct in 80/80 audited trials.
- The same authentic donor, tool schema, evidence-record constructor, source
  probe, final assessment, new case, and history distance were used throughout.
- Behavior was measured after source assessment, as in Takeover 07, while the
  actor field was the only evidence difference within each channel.

## Primary result

- Active retrieval: self 2/20 (10.0%; Wilson 95% CI 2.8%--30.1%) versus other
  1/20 (5.0%; 0.9%--23.6%), Fisher p=1.0.
- Passive delivery: self 0/20 (0%; 0%--16.1%) versus other 3/20 (15.0%;
  5.2%--36.0%), Fisher p=.231.
- Approximate Breslow-Day actor-by-channel diagnostic: chi-square=3.19,
  p=.074. This does not meet the pre-registered interaction requirement.
- Within self, active versus passive p=.487; within other, p=.605.

Thus neither actor contrast supports a self-ownership adoption advantage, and
active retrieval does not explain Takeover 07. The passive reversal is small,
non-significant, and contrary to prediction; it is not promoted as a finding.

## Behavioral and component checks

- Native/no-audit complete procedure was 4/20, so the donor history retained a
  nonzero behavior window during this run.
- Audited conditions ranged from 0/20 to 3/20, suggesting general audit/response
  compression rather than an actor-specific gate.
- Goodwill exactly matched complete sequence in every trial.
- Redundant `get_user` was 20/20 in every cell and is excluded as a
  source-sensitive outcome.

## 11/11 statistical fallacy scan

1. **Tiny samples / power:** n=20 narrows uncertainty relative to Takeover 07
   but cannot exclude small or moderate interactions; it does reject replication
   of the earlier observed effect size.
2. **Multiple comparisons:** the active actor contrast was primary; passive and
   within-actor tests are mechanism diagnostics and are all reported.
3. **Pseudoreplication:** repeated trials share one donor and task, so no
   donor-population claim is made.
4. **Non-independence / leakage:** donor and target remain in customer service;
   cross-domain generalization is not claimed.
5. **Selective reporting:** the passive reversal, non-significant interaction,
   component ceiling, and all nulls are retained.
6. **Post-hoc subgrouping:** channel, actor, metrics, sample size, and decision
   rules were specified before execution.
7. **Dichotomization:** exact sequence is accompanied by goodwill, ritual, and
   continuous procedure score.
8. **Mean-only reporting:** raw counts, Wilson intervals, tests, episodes, and
   executable analysis are preserved.
9. **Correlation as causation:** source actor and delivery channel were
   experimentally assigned; causal language is limited to this prompt design.
10. **Null equals no effect:** results reject the predicted large, stable gate;
    they do not prove exact equivalence or absence of small effects.
11. **Measurement / floor-ceiling effects:** audited exact rates are low and the
    ritual is at ceiling. The no-audit window and perfect manipulations are
    reported, but small actor effects could be hidden by compression.

## Decision

1. Reject active retrieval as the supported mechanism.
2. Downgrade Takeover 07 from B-grade mechanism evidence to a C-grade brittle
   clue until the exact original procedure independently replicates.
3. Run one exact, higher-n Takeover 07 replication. If it fails, freeze this
   actor-gating branch; if it succeeds, isolate free evidence elaboration versus
   fixed acknowledgment in a new randomized study.
